The IEEE 30-bus system has a meshed 132\,kV transmission level and a 33\,kV distribution level. Bus~1 is the only reference bus, at~$1.0\angle0^{\circ}$\,pu at the fundamental; the other generator buses are voltage-controlled buses, and no bus is an ideal harmonic ground. At the harmonics, the generators are represented by their subtransient impedance, with a short-circuit power of~2\,GVA for the generators at buses~1 and~2 and~0.2\,GVA for the others, at a reactance-to-resistance ratio of~20, as in~\cite{vanacker_impact_2025}. The line charging of the original data is removed, so that the base network is purely inductive, as the overhead-line case of~\cite{vanacker_impact_2025}%
. %
 The fundamental operating point follows from an ac optimal power flow. The harmonics $\HarmonicSet = \{5,7,11,13,17,19,23,25\}$, %
 are limited by the individual and total harmonic distortion planning levels and the root-mean-square voltage and current limits. The harmonic units are the first~$N$ loads, with $N = 21$ unless stated otherwise, under the absolute equality fairness criterion, at a risk level~$\varepsilon = 0.05$. All results are in per-unit on a 100\,MVA basis%
.

\subsection{Equivalence of the Methods}
\label{sec:equivalence}

Table~\ref{tab:equivalence} compares the methods of Section~\ref{sec:mathematical_framework} on the base network. When every angle range collapses to its mean, the uncertainty vanishes and the second-order cone program~\eqref{eq:socp} reproduces the deterministic model of~\cite{vanacker_hosting_2026}. With uncertainty, the second-order cone program and the general quadratically constrained program agree to within~$10^{-7}$ with the empirical risk factor%
; the former is solved with \textsc{Mosek}, the latter with the local solver \textsc{Ipopt}, which limits it to~$N = 4$. Finally, the hosting capacity reduced to the individual harmonic voltage limits~\eqref{eq:reduced_hosting} equals the deterministic model at the mean angles and, for the exact~95\,\% quantile, the second-order cone program with the empirical risk factor, which confirms that these limits bind and that the reduced metrics of Section~\ref{sec:levers} describe the full model.

\subsection{Levers for Harmonic Recombination}
\label{sec:levers}

With a single hosting capacity per harmonic and binding individual harmonic voltage limits, as in all cases below, the hosting capacity at harmonic~$\Harmonic$ is set by the tightest bus,
\begin{IEEEeqnarray}{ r C l }
    \HostingCapacity_{\Harmonic} &=& \min_{\Node \in \NodeSet} \frac{U^{\text{ihd}}_{\Node,\Harmonic}\,|\ComplexVoltage_{\Node,1}|}{A_{\Node,\Harmonic}}, \qquad c_{\Node\Unit,\Harmonic} = Z_{\Harmonic,\Node\Node(\Unit)}\,e^{\im\phi_{\Unit,\Harmonic}}, \label{eq:reduced_hosting}
\end{IEEEeqnarray}
\suggested{where~$A_{\Node,\Harmonic}$ aggregates the unit contributions~$c_{\Node\Unit,\Harmonic}$ per unit of current by one of three rules: (i)~arithmetic summation~$\sum_{\Unit}|c_{\Node\Unit,\Harmonic}|$, i.e., no recombination; (ii)~the mean angle, $|\sum_{\Unit}c_{\Node\Unit,\Harmonic}|$ at~$\Expectation[\bar\omega]$; and (iii)~the 95\,\% chance bound, i.e., the exact $(1-\varepsilon)$-quantile of~$|\sum_{\Unit}c_{\Node\Unit,\Harmonic}|$. The equivalent summation exponent~$\alpha^{\text{eq}}_{\Harmonic}$ is the exponent~$\alpha$ for which the summation law of IEC~61000-3-6~\cite{noauthor_iec61000-3-6_2008}, $A_{\Node,\Harmonic} = (\sum_{\Unit}|c_{\Node\Unit,\Harmonic}|^{\alpha})^{1/\alpha}$, reproduces rule~(iii); its indicative values are~1.4 for~$5 \leq \Harmonic \leq 10$ and~2 for~$\Harmonic > 10$.}

\suggested{Two networks and three converter populations are compared. Besides the purely inductive base network, all 33\,kV lines are replaced by underground cables (155\,km in total), \emph{cables at mv}, followed by the same re-dispatch. The cables place resonances close to the 7th, 13th and 25th harmonic, whereas the base network has none. The 21 units are either all diode bridges, Table~\ref{tab:beta_phase_params_diode_to25}, all thyristor bridges, Table~\ref{tab:beta_phase_params_pc_to25}, with the firing delay sampled independently per unit, or a mix of both, alternating over the units, i.e., 11 diode and 10 thyristor bridges.}

\suggested{Fig.~\ref{fig:populations} shows the result. The cables lower the hosting capacity under arithmetic summation by~19\,\%, but the 95\,\% hosting capacity changes by only~$-5$ to~$+4$\,\%, as near a resonance the transfer impedances from the units to a bus rotate apart, which spreads their contributions. A single converter technology recombines little in the base network: the 95\,\% hosting capacity lies~7\,\% above arithmetic summation for the diode bridges and~21\,\% for the thyristor bridges, and~37 and~51\,\% with the cables (Fig.~\ref{fig:populations}a). The equivalent summation exponent rises with the harmonic order as the angle spread grows, to~1.10 for the diode bridges and~1.31 for the more widely spread thyristor bridges in the base network (Fig.~\ref{fig:populations}b). With the cables, the network rotation cancels the narrowly spread diode contributions, whose exponent reaches~1.39--1.43 at the 23rd and 25th harmonic (Fig.~\ref{fig:populations}c), whereas the thyristor bridges stay at~1.32--1.33, as their phasors are already nearly uniform, with a mean phasor length of~0.09 at the 25th harmonic. A mix of both technologies recombines substantially at the low orders, by~57\,\% over arithmetic summation in the base network and~85\,\% with the cables. The thyristor bridges lag the diode bridges by about~20$^{\circ}$ at the fundamental, and by about~$\Harmonic \cdot 20^{\circ}$ at harmonic~$\Harmonic$. Consequently, their mean angles differ by~150--154$^{\circ}$ at the 7th and 11th harmonic, where the exponent reaches~1.38--1.47, and by~92--107$^{\circ}$ at the 5th and 13th, where it reaches~1.10--1.30. Beyond, the mean angles differ by only~8--65$^{\circ}$ and the thyristor phasors are short, so the mix lies between both technologies in the base network, at~1.14--1.19, whereas with the cables its diode bridges carry the cancellation by the network, up to~1.55 at the 23rd harmonic. The high-order exponent remains below the indicative~2.0, and the summation law overestimates the hosting capacity by a factor of~1.9--2.3 for one technology and~1.5--1.7 for the mix.}

\suggested{The aggregation rule matters as much as the population. With one technology, all units share their mean angle, so the mean angle reflects the network alone and gives the same hosting capacity for both technologies. It ignores that the spread of the angles shortens the mean phasor of each unit, which dominates where the contributions nearly align, e.g., for the thyristor bridges in the base network, 14\,\% above the mean angle. However, this also adds variance, which dominates where they nearly cancel. As a consequence, the mean angle rule overestimates the mix by~29--38\,\% and, with the cables, one technology by~11--22\,\%, and by more than a factor of two at the 23rd and 25th harmonic. The chance constraint rule is then the only one of the three rules that is both safe and captures the cancellation.}

\subsection{Computational Tractability}
\label{sec:tractability}

With the empirical risk factor, i.e., including its calibration by Monte Carlo simulation with 20\,000 samples per iteration and the two to three cone programs until convergence, every case of Section~\ref{sec:levers} solves in~1.9--2.2\,s, of which the solver takes at most~\changed{0.19}\,s, as the program keeps two variables per harmonic. From 4 to 21 units, the cardinality~$|\PCESet|$ of the basis grows quadratically, from 15 to 253, while the total time grows from~\changed{0.4 to~2.2}\,s, as the sampling of the calibration takes the largest share. The number of harmonics and the network enter only through the precomputed scalars and the sampled quantities\changed{: from one to eight harmonics, the total time grows about linearly, from~0.3 to~2.0\,s}, so even one random variable per unit remains tractable.

\subsection{Comparison with the Summation Law and the Literature}
\label{sec:comparison}

\suggested{The exponent close to one for one technology at the low orders agrees with the measurements of Wang et al.\ in residential distribution systems, where low-order harmonic currents from different homes add up with little cancellation~\cite{wang_characteristics_2017}, and with the caution of Santos et al.\ that the indicative exponents may overestimate the aggregate of correlated sources~\cite{santos_considerations_2015}, whereas Cho et al.\ found that deterministic emission limits can drive the voltage distortion to twice the planning levels~\cite{cho_analysis_2019}. The rise of the exponent with the harmonic order agrees qualitatively with the cancellation that Wang et al.\ measured mainly at the higher orders, but it remains below the indicative~2.0. The high-order exponent and the harmonics at which the mix recombines most are the results most sensitive to the converter models of Section~\ref{sec:uncertainty}, e.g., whether the firing delay of the thyristor bridges is common to all units, and to the network resonances.}
