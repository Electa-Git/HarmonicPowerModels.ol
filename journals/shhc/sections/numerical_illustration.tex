\section{Numerical Illustration}
\label{sec:numerical_illustration}

This section quantifies how much harmonic recombination the stochastic harmonic hosting capacity captures, and under which conditions it occurs. First, Section~\ref{sec:test_system} describes the test system and the capacitive scenarios. Second, Section~\ref{sec:validation} validates the formulation. Third, Sections~\ref{sec:single_type} and~\ref{sec:mixed_types} present the results for a single converter type and for a mix of converter types, respectively. Finally, Section~\ref{sec:conditions} summarizes the conditions for recombination. All results are in per unit on a 100\,MVA basis.

\subsection{Test System and Scenarios}
\label{sec:test_system}

The IEEE 30-bus system is used, which has transmission (132\,kV) and distribution (33\,kV) levels and, unlike radial feeders, a meshed structure. Bus~1 is the only reference bus, at~$1.0\angle0^{\circ}$\,p.u.\ at the fundamental; the other generator buses are PV buses, and no bus is an ideal harmonic ground. At the harmonics, the generators are represented by their subtransient impedance, with a short-circuit power of~2\,GVA for the generators at buses~1 and~2 and~0.2\,GVA for the others, at an~$X/R$ ratio of~20, as in~\cite{vanacker_impact_2025}; together with the line charging, these form the only harmonic return path. The fundamental operating point follows from an AC optimal power flow with bus~1 fixed at~1.0\,p.u.\ and the branch ratings enforced. The harmonics~$\HarmonicSet = \{5,7,11,13\}$, characteristic of the 6-pulse converter of Section~\ref{sec:uncertainty}, are limited by the individual and total harmonic distortion planning levels and the root-mean-square voltage and current limits. The harmonic units are the first~$N$ loads, with $N \in \{8, 21\}$, under the absolute equality fairness criterion, and the risk level is~$\varepsilon = 0.05$.

Four network scenarios are considered, each re-dispatched with the same optimal power flow, with the upper voltage limit at~1.05\,p.u.\ to retain root-mean-square headroom:
\begin{enumerate*}
    \item[(i)] \emph{base}: the network without its original capacitor banks;
    \item[(ii)] \emph{banks}: the original banks of~19\,Mvar at bus~10 and~4.3\,Mvar at bus~24 restored;
    \item[(iii)] \emph{cable33}: all 33\,kV lines replaced by underground cables (155\,km in total); and
    \item[(iv)] \emph{cable132c}: all 132\,kV lines replaced by cables (588\,km), with~80\,\% of their charging compensated by shunt reactors.
\end{enumerate*}
The cable route lengths follow from the overhead-line reactance, and typical cable parameters are used. Without compensation, the 132\,kV cables cannot be operated within the branch ratings. Fig.~\ref{fig:freqscan} shows the resulting driving-point impedance: the banks place a parallel resonance at~$\Harmonic = 5.0$, the 33\,kV cables at~$\Harmonic \approx 6$, and the compensated 132\,kV cables lower the impedance and shift the resonances to~$\Harmonic \approx 2.5$ and~$6.5$.

Two converter populations are compared: \emph{A}, all units of the converter type of Section~\ref{sec:uncertainty}; and \emph{AB}, alternately type~A and the phase-controlled type~B of Section~\ref{sec:converter_types}, whose firing delay is common to all type-B units. Population~A has~$N$ germs and population~AB has~$N+1$, i.e., $|\PCESet| = 253$ and~$276$ for $N=21$.

\subsection{Validation}
\label{sec:validation}

Three checks support the results. First, when all angle ranges collapse to their mean, the second-order cone program~\eqref{eq:socp} reproduces the deterministic model of~\cite{vanacker_hosting_2026} to within~$10^{-6}\,\%$, both in hosting capacity and in the injected current and bus voltage phasors. Second, on the industrial network of~\cite{vanacker_hosting_2026} with four units, the second-order cone program and the general quadratically constrained program agree to within~0.05\,\% for all three risk factors, and to within~1\,\% with a type germ; the general program is solved with a local solver and is used for this check only, as it scales poorly. Third, the hosting capacity reduced to the binding constraints~\eqref{eq:reduced_hosting} equals the deterministic model to within~$3\cdot10^{-6}\,\%$ in every case of Table~\ref{tab:results}, which confirms that the individual harmonic voltage limits bind throughout.

\subsection{Single Converter Type}
\label{sec:single_type}

Table~\ref{tab:results} lists the total hosting capacity~$\sum_{\Harmonic} I_{\Harmonic}$ under the aggregation rules of Section~\ref{sec:recombination_metrics} and for the full second-order cone program with the three risk factors. For population~A, the 95\,\% chance bound lies 0--5\,\% below the deterministic angle and 3--26\,\% above the robust bound. The equivalent summation exponent, Fig.~\ref{fig:alpha}(a), stays at~1.01--1.04 for the 5th and 7th harmonic and reaches 1.02--1.34 for the 11th and 13th harmonic, far below the indicative 1.4 and 2.0 of IEC~61000-3-6. The contributions of a single converter type arrive nearly in phase at the binding buses: the transfer impedances of an inductive network are all close to~$90^{\circ}$, and a single converter type shares one mean angle. Only the higher harmonics, with the widest angle ranges and the largest rotation~$\Harmonic\,\theta_{\Node}$ from the local angle reference, recombine noticeably. Consequently, the summation law with the indicative exponents overestimates the hosting capacity by a factor of 1.8--2.4. This agrees with field measurements reporting near-arithmetic summation of low-order harmonics~\cite{wang_characteristics_2017} and with the caution of~\cite{santos_considerations_2015} that the indicative exponents may overestimate the aggregate.

The capacitive elements change the hosting capacity mainly through the network impedance: the banks reduce it by~21\,\% through the 5th-harmonic resonance, the 33\,kV cables by~37\,\%, while the compensated 132\,kV cables raise it by~67\,\% by stiffening the network. Their effect on recombination is limited to the higher harmonics, where the resonances rotate the transfer impedances apart.

Among the risk factors, the empirical factor reproduces the exact 95\,\% value, the Gaussian factor lies within~0.3\,\%, and the distribution-free Cantelli bound costs~2--8\,\%. The deterministic angle is not a safe reference: depending on the scenario, up to about 40\,\% of the angle realizations admit less hosting capacity than the deterministic angle at the mean.

\subsection{Mixed Converter Types}
\label{sec:mixed_types}

Mixing converter types with different mean angles is the condition under which substantial recombination occurs. For population~AB, the 95\,\% hosting capacity is~28--35\,\% above population~A for $N = 21$, Table~\ref{tab:results}, and 9--46\,\% for $N = 8$. The equivalent summation exponent of the 5th and 7th harmonic rises to~1.17--1.30, Fig.~\ref{fig:alpha}(b), and up to~1.48 for $N = 8$, i.e., close to the indicative~1.4, whereas the 11th and 13th harmonic remain at~1.02--1.36, below the indicative~2.0. With mixed types, the deterministic angle overestimates the 95\,\% hosting capacity by up to~73\,\% for $N = 21$, as the angles at the mean of the type germ happen to cancel more than the distribution does. The Gaussian risk factor overestimates the 95\,\% value by~3--6\,\%, as the common type germ makes the output distribution far from Gaussian; the empirical factor remains within~1.5\,\%.

Fig.~\ref{fig:sweeps} shows the sensitivity of the gain over population~A to the type-B assumptions. For a fixed firing delay, Fig.~\ref{fig:sweeps}(a), the gain peaks where $\Harmonic\,\alpha_{\text{f}}$ approaches~$180^{\circ}$ for one of the harmonics, i.e., where type~B opposes type~A, and vanishes for small delays. The gain is largest for a balanced mix, Fig.~\ref{fig:sweeps}(b), and falls when the firing delay itself is uncertain, Fig.~\ref{fig:sweeps}(c); it nevertheless retains~38--48\,\% for a delay range of~$60^{\circ}$. Recombination through technology diversity is therefore a regime to be identified from data on the actual converter mix, rather than a default to be assumed.

\subsection{Conditions for Harmonic Recombination}
\label{sec:conditions}

The results identify the conditions under which harmonic recombination raises the hosting capacity:
\begin{enumerate*}
    \item[(i)] differences in mean angle between units, from different converter technologies, which is the dominant mechanism, provided the mix is balanced and the angle difference approaches opposition at a binding harmonic;
    \item[(ii)] the rotation~$\Harmonic\,\theta_{\Node}$ of the local angle reference, which grows with the harmonic order and with the spread of the fundamental voltage angles; and
    \item[(iii)] capacitive elements that rotate the transfer impedances apart around a resonance, mainly at the higher harmonics.
\end{enumerate*}
Uncertainty around a common mean angle does not create recombination by itself: it costs hosting capacity relative to the deterministic angle, and its benefit is the gain over a robust bound that a chance constraint offers.
