% Real networks host a mix of converter technologies, whose harmonic current angles differ in their mean. As a second type, a phase-controlled version of the same 6-pulse converter is considered, i.e., a thyristor rectifier operated at a firing delay~$\alpha_{\text{f}}$. Delaying the firing delays the whole current waveform, which rotates the $\Harmonic$-th harmonic phasor of phase~$a$ by~$-\Harmonic\,\alpha_{\text{f}}$ relative to the uncontrolled converter, while the dispersion caused by the uncertain inductances is retained. The firing delay is set by the operating point, e.g., the demanded dc voltage, and is therefore common to all units of this type and uncertain at the planning stage.

Real networks host a mix of converter technologies. As a second type, the same line-commutated converter unit is equipped with thyristors and operated in phase-controlled mode, resulting in firing delay~$\alpha_{\text{f}}$. Each unit regulates its dc current to a load-dependent reference, assumed common to all units of this type but uncertain at the planning stage from a operator perspective. The reference is expressed as the firing delay~$\alpha_{\text{op}}$ of a unit with the mean commutation inductance~$\bar{L}_{ac}$, so that $\alpha_{\text{f}} = \alpha_{\text{op}}$ for $L_{ac} = \bar{L}_{ac}$. In the absence of operating data, $\alpha_{\text{op}}$ is sampled, as $L_{ac}$ and $L_{dc}$, from a normal distribution with mean of $30^{\circ}$ and standard deviation of $5^{\circ}$, truncated to $[15^{\circ}, 45^{\circ}]$. The firing delay at each sample is determined by,
\begin{equation}
    \cos\alpha_{\text{f}} = \frac{I_{dc}^{\text{ref}}\left(R_{dc} + \frac{3}{\pi}\omega_1 L_{ac}\right)}{V_{d0}}, 
    I_{dc}^{\text{ref}} = \frac{V_{d0}\cos\alpha_{\text{op}}}{R_{dc} + \frac{3}{\pi}\omega_1 \bar{L}_{ac}},
\end{equation}
where $V_{d0} = 3\sqrt{2}\,V_{LL}/\pi$ and $V_{LL}$ is the line-to-line voltage at ac terminal. At a given current reference, a larger $L_{ac}$ therefore requires earlier firing to compensate for the increased commutation voltage drop.

Joint Monte Carlo sampling of $L_{ac}$, $L_{dc}$ and $\alpha_{\text{op}}$ ($N = 5000$) yields the harmonic current phase angle distributions for the phase-controlled converter. Following the same uncertainty quantification procedure as for the diode-based converter, the simulated angles are unwrapped about their respective circular means and normalized, and a shared Beta distribution is fitted to the pooled normalized samples. The resulting shared shape parameters, harmonic-specific locations and scales, and fit errors are listed in Table~\ref{tab:beta_phase_params_pc}. The histograms from the Monte Carlo simulation and the fitted probability densities are compared in~Fig.~\ref{fig:Shared_Beta_Fit_pc}. As summarized in Table~\ref{tab:fund_angle},the lag of the fundamental current behind the terminal reference voltage~$v_a$ ($v_a = V_a\cos(\omega_1 t - 90^{\circ})$) increases from $9.43^{\circ}$-$16.70^{\circ}$ for the diode-based converter to $20.15^{\circ}$-$47.26^{\circ}$ for the phase-controlled converter. The firing delay~$\alpha_{\text{f}}$ shifts the current waveform later in time and introduces a phase shift of~$-h\alpha_{\text{f}}$ to the $h$-th harmonic. Since uncertainty in operating reference introduces additional order-dependent spread in harmonic phase angles of the phase-controlled converter, the distribution ranges are wider than those of the diode-based converter for every harmonic considered, as indicated in~Fig.~\ref{fig:Shared_Beta_Fit} and~Fig.~\ref{fig:Shared_Beta_Fit_pc}.
