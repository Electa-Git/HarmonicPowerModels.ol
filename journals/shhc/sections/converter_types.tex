Real networks host a mix of converter technologies, whose harmonic current angles differ in their mean. As a second type, the same 6-pulse converter unit is equipped with thyristors and operated at phase-controlled mode, resulting a firing delay~$\alpha_{\text{f}}$. Each unit controls its dc current to a reference that is needed for the load and is therefore common to all units of this type and uncertain at the planning stage. The reference is expressed as the firing delay~$\alpha_{\text{op}}$ of a unit with the mean commutation inductance~$\bar{L}_{ac}$, so that $\alpha_{\text{f}} = \alpha_{\text{op}}$ for $L_{ac} = \bar{L}_{ac}$. As no operating data are available, $\alpha_{\text{op}}$ is sampled, like $L_{ac}$ and $L_{dc}$, from a normal distribution centred in its range $[15^{\circ}, 45^{\circ}]$, truncated at the bounds. Each unit then fires at
\begin{equation}
    \cos\alpha_{\text{f}} = \frac{I_{dc}^{\text{ref}}\left(R_{dc} + \frac{3}{\pi}\omega_1 L_{ac}\right)}{V_{d0}}, \quad
    I_{dc}^{\text{ref}} = \frac{V_{d0}\cos\alpha_{\text{op}}}{R_{dc} + \frac{3}{\pi}\omega_1 \bar{L}_{ac}},
\end{equation}
with $V_{d0} = 3\sqrt{2}\,V_{LL}/\pi$, i.e., a larger $L_{ac}$ is compensated by an earlier firing. Sampling $L_{ac}$, $L_{dc}$ and $\alpha_{\text{op}}$ jointly ($N = 5000$) increases the lag of the fundamental current behind~$v_a$ from $9.4^{\circ}$--$16.7^{\circ}$ to $20.2^{\circ}$--$47.8^{\circ}$. Delaying the firing delays the current waveform, so the $\Harmonic$-th angle decreases with~$\alpha_{\text{f}}$. A shared Beta distribution, with parameters listed in Table~\ref{tab:beta_phase_params_pc}, still describes them well.
