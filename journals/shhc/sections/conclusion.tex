\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper presents a stochastic harmonic hosting capacity method that accounts for the uncertain angle of the harmonic currents of power-electronic interfaced units, and thereby for harmonic recombination. The angles of a 6-pulse line-commutated converter are characterized through a harmonic state-space model and represented by one Beta-distributed random variable per unit%
%, and one per converter type for a common firing delay
, referenced to each unit's terminal voltage and projected exactly onto the polynomial chaos basis. For fairness criteria that fix the ratios between the allocations of the units, the general non-convex problem reduces to a second-order cone program with one pair of variables per harmonic, which equals the general program and the deterministic model when the uncertainty vanishes.

Capacitive elements change the hosting capacity by~$-48$ to~$+62\,\%$ and raise the gain over arithmetic summation from 4 to~7--12\,\%. Substantial recombination requires a mix of converter types: four or more types raise the 95\,\% hosting capacity by~80\,\% or more and bring the low-order exponent to the indicative~1.4, while the higher orders stay below~2.0. The choice of the stochastic bound is a lever of comparable order, and every case solves in less than~2\,s.
On the IEEE 30-bus system, recombination among units of one converter technology is limited: for diode as well as thyristor bridges, the equivalent summation exponent stays between~1.01 and~1.22, and the indicative exponents of IEC~61000-3-6 overestimate the hosting capacity by a factor of~2.0--2.8. Underground cables at 33\,kV lower the hosting capacity by~44--48\,\% and raise the gain over arithmetic summation, e.g., from 4 to~12\,\% for diode bridges. Substantial recombination requires a mix of converter technologies: a mix of diode and thyristor bridges raises the 95\,\% hosting capacity by~62--88\,\% over arithmetic summation and brings the exponent of the 7th harmonic to the indicative~1.4, while the higher orders stay below~2.0. For such a mix, the mean angle overestimates the hosting capacity by up to~53\,\%, so the chance constraint is needed. %

%{\color{red}Future work will extend the method to the zero-sequence harmonics, i.e., the harmonic orders that are multiples of three. The second-order cone program carries over unchanged, since each harmonic enters only through its own transfer impedances; the extension lies in the network model and in the definition of the units. First, the zero-sequence network requires the winding configuration and earthing of the transformers, generators and shunt elements: delta windings split it into separately earthed parts, and the earth return makes the line and cable impedances depend on the earth resistivity. Second, a three-phase three-wire unit injects no zero-sequence current, so only units connected through an earthed path host these harmonics. Third, the transformer vector groups either block the zero-sequence harmonics or pass them without phase shift, so the angle spread that the transformers give the other harmonics is absent, and less recombination is expected.}
