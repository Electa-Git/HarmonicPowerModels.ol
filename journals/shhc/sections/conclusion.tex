\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper presents a stochastic harmonic hosting capacity method that accounts for the uncertain angle of the harmonic currents of power-electronic interfaced units, and thereby for harmonic recombination. The angles of a 6-pulse line-commutated converter are characterized through a harmonic state-space model and represented by one Beta-distributed random variable per unit, and one per converter type for a common firing delay, referenced to each unit's terminal voltage and projected exactly onto the polynomial chaos basis. For fairness criteria that fix the ratios between the allocations of the units, the general non-convex problem reduces to a second-order cone program with one pair of variables per harmonic, which equals the general program and the deterministic model when the uncertainty vanishes.

On the IEEE 30-bus system, recombination among units of one converter type is limited: the equivalent summation exponent stays close to one, and the indicative exponents of IEC~61000-3-6 overestimate the hosting capacity by a factor of~1.8--2.8. Capacitive elements change the hosting capacity by~$-48$ to~$+62\,\%$ and raise the gain over arithmetic summation from 4 to~7--12\,\%. Substantial recombination requires a mix of converter types: four or more types raise the 95\,\% hosting capacity by~80\,\% or more and bring the low-order exponent to the indicative~1.4, while the higher orders stay below~2.0. The choice of the stochastic bound is a lever of comparable order, and every case solves in less than~2\,s.

Future work will characterize the firing delay of phase-controlled converters from harmonic state-space data and extend the uncertainty to background distortion and network parameters.
