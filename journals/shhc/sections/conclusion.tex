\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper presents a stochastic harmonic hosting capacity method that accounts for the uncertain angle of the harmonic currents injected by power-electronic interfaced units, and thereby for harmonic recombination. The angle uncertainty of a 6-pulse line-commutated converter is characterized through a harmonic state-space model and Monte Carlo simulation, and represented by one Beta-distributed random variable per unit, which captures the strong dependence of its angles across the harmonic orders. A random variable shared by all units of a converter type represents a common firing delay at little extra cost. The angles are referenced to the fundamental voltage at each unit's terminal, and their trigonometric functions are projected onto the polynomial chaos basis exactly by quadrature. The general problem is a non-convex quadratically constrained program; under the absolute equality fairness criterion it reduces to a second-order cone program with one pair of variables per harmonic, independent of the network size and the cardinality of the basis, which equals the general program and reduces exactly to the deterministic model when the angle ranges collapse.

The numerical illustration on the IEEE 30-bus system shows that harmonic recombination is limited when all units are of one converter type: the contributions arrive nearly in phase at the binding buses, the equivalent summation exponent stays close to one at every harmonic, and the indicative exponents of IEC~61000-3-6 overestimate the hosting capacity by a factor of~1.8--2.8, in line with field measurements of low-order harmonics. Capacitive elements change the hosting capacity strongly, by~$-48$ to~$+62\,\%$, through the network impedance and its resonances, but hardly change recombination. Substantial recombination requires units with different mean angles, such as a mix of converter technologies: four or more types raise the 95\,\% hosting capacity by~80\,\% or more and bring the equivalent exponent of the low-order harmonics to the indicative~1.4, while the higher orders stay below the indicative~2.0. The choice of the stochastic bound is a lever of comparable order: the Cantelli bound is safe but costs up to~38\,\% of hosting capacity with several converter types, the Gaussian quantile is then unsafe, and an empirically calibrated risk factor recovers the exact chance constraint.

Future work will replace the working assumptions on the phase-controlled converter by a harmonic state-space characterization of its firing delay, fit the trigonometric coefficients directly from such data, and extend the uncertainty to background distortion and network parameters.
