\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper presents a stochastic harmonic hosting capacity method that accounts for harmonic recombination through the uncertain angle of the harmonic currents, represented by one Beta-distributed random variable per unit, characterized by a harmonic state-space model and referenced to the terminal voltage of the unit. For fairness criteria that fix the ratios between the allocations, the problem reduces to a second-order cone program with one pair of variables per harmonic, which recovers the deterministic model when the uncertainty vanishes.

On the IEEE 30-bus system, up to the 25th harmonic, units of one converter technology recombine little at the low orders, and the indicative exponents of IEC~61000-3-6 overestimate their hosting capacity by a factor of about two. The exponent grows with the harmonic order, and cable resonances add recombination through the network. A mix of diode and thyristor bridges raises the 95\,\% hosting capacity by~57--85\,\% and the exponent to about~1.4 where the mean angles of both technologies nearly oppose each other, but it stays below~2.0 at the higher orders. The mean angle overestimates the hosting capacity wherever contributions cancel, by more than a factor of two at some harmonics, so the chance constraint is needed. Every case solves in about~2\,s.
