\section{Mathematical Framework}
\label{sec:mathematical_framework}

This section presents the mathematical framework for the stochastic harmonic hosting capacity problem of a phase-balanced three-phase power system, considering uncertainty on the angle of the harmonic current injected by the connected harmonic units. To this end, the formulation for the deterministic harmonic hosting capacity presented in~\cite{vanacker_hosting_2026} is extended. For brevity, the original formulation is not repeated in this section, rather the relevant variables and constraints are highlighted and transformed to their stochastic counterpart. For the full stochastic formulation, the reader is referred to the supplementary material.

\subsection{Background on Polynomial Chaos Expansion}
\label{sec:polynomial_chaos_expansion}

This section introduces the necessary background on polynomial chaos expansion, a method for representing random variables in terms of polynomial functions. Concretely, a vector of input random variables, e.g., harmonic current angles, is propagated through the set of constraints of the harmonic hosting capacity problem to obtain a vector of unknown output random variables, e.g., harmonic bus voltages. For a detailed treatment of the subject, the reader is referred to~\cite{sullivan_introduction_2015}.

\subsubsection{Uncertainty Quantification}
It is assumed that the stochastic input is captured by a real-valued finite-variance stochastic germ~$\bar{\omega} = [\omega_{1},...,\omega_{N}]^{\text{T}}$ of independent random variables. Optimization solvers are unable to handle random variables directly, necessitating a deterministic equivalent of the stochastic problem. For this purpose, polynomial chaos expansion approximates any real-valued random variable~$\RVX$ of finite variance that is a function of the germ~$\bar{\omega}$ as a linear combination~$\ApproxRVX$ of the orthogonal polynomial basis~$[\Polynoom_{\PCEIndex}(\bar{\omega})]_{\PCEIndex \in \PCESet}$, where $\PCESet\subseteq\NaturalNumberSet_{0}$, as,
\begin{IEEEeqnarray}{ r C l }
    \ApproxRVX &=& \sum_{\PCEIndex \in \PCESet} \CoefficientX_{\PCEIndex} \cdot \Polynoom_{\PCEIndex} =
    \sum_{\PCEIndex \in \PCESet} \frac{\langle \RVX, \Polynoom_{\PCEIndex} \rangle}{\langle \Polynoom_{\PCEIndex}, \Polynoom_{\PCEIndex} \rangle} \cdot \Polynoom_{\PCEIndex}, \label{eq:gpc_expansion}
\end{IEEEeqnarray}
where~$\CoefficientX_{\PCEIndex} \in \RealNumberSet$ denote the polynomial chaos expansion coefficients and $\langle \cdot,\cdot \rangle$ denotes the inner product with respect to the probability measure of the germ. Following the Askey scheme~\cite{sullivan_introduction_2015}, each Beta-distributed random variable corresponds to the Jacobi family of orthogonal polynomials; the multivariate basis is the tensor product of the univariate ones, truncated to total degree~$\delta$, and is constructed numerically with \textsc{PolyChaos.jl}~\cite{muhlpfordt_polychaos_2020}.

\subsubsection{Uncertainty Projection onto Equality Constraints}
Through polynomial chaos expansion, any stochastic equality constraint is equivalently represented by $|\PCESet|$ deterministic equality constraints. The cardinality of the model~$|\PCESet|$ is determined by the dimension of the germ and the highest polynomial degree as,
\begin{IEEEeqnarray}{ r C l }
    |\PCESet| &=& \frac{(|\bar{\omega}| + \delta)!}{|\bar{\omega}|!\,\delta!}, \label{eq:cardinality}
\end{IEEEeqnarray}
where~$\delta$ denotes the maximum degree of the polynomial basis functions. \removable{The truncation error~$\norm{\RVX-\ApproxRVX}$ decays to zero for~$|\PCESet| \to \infty$.}

The Galerkin projection forms a system of equations for the stochastic modes~(Chapter~12 in~\cite{sullivan_introduction_2015}). For the harmonic hosting capacity problem, two operations stand out: summation and multiplication. For three approximated random variables~$\ApproxRVX$,~$\ApproxRVY$ and~$\ApproxRVZ$ defined on the same basis, the Galerkin projection, denoted by~$\gp$, of both operations is,
\begin{IEEEeqnarray}{ l C l l}
    \ApproxRVZ = \ApproxRVX + \ApproxRVY &\gp& \CoefficientZ_{\PCEIndex} = \CoefficientX_{\PCEIndex} + \CoefficientY_{\PCEIndex},~&\forall \PCEIndex \in \PCESet,~ \label{eq:gp_sum} \\
    \ApproxRVZ = \ApproxRVX \cdot \ApproxRVY &\gp& \CoefficientZ_{\PCEIndex} = \mkern-15mu \sum_{\bar{\kappa} \in P^{*}(\PCESet,2)} \mkern-15mu M_{\bar{\kappa},\PCEIndex} \,(\CoefficientX_{\bar{\kappa}_{1}} \cdot \CoefficientY_{\bar{\kappa}_{2}}),\quad&\forall \PCEIndex \in \PCESet,~ \label{eq:gp_product}
\end{IEEEeqnarray}
where~$P^{*}(\PCESet,2)$ denotes the 2-tuples of~$\PCESet$ and the multiplication tensor,
\begin{IEEEeqnarray}{ r C l }
    M_{\bar{\kappa},\PCEIndex} = \frac{\langle \Polynoom_{\bar{\kappa}_{1}}\Polynoom_{\bar{\kappa}_{2}},\Polynoom_{k} \rangle}{\langle \Polynoom_{k},\Polynoom_{k}\rangle}, \label{eq_m_tensor_def}
\end{IEEEeqnarray}
is computed ahead of time. For $\PCEIndex=0$, orthogonality implies that $M_{\bar{\kappa},0}$ is diagonal with strictly positive entries; for $\PCEIndex \neq 0$, the tensor has mixed-sign entries, and the matrix $[M_{\bar{\kappa},k}]_{\bar\kappa}$ is generally indefinite.

\subsubsection{Chance-Constraint Reformulation of Inequality Constraints}
All harmonic limits in this work are upper bounds. A chance constraint ensures that the probability of~$\RVX$ exceeding a deterministic limit~$x^{\text{max}}$ is below a risk level~$\varepsilon$, i.e., $\Probability(\RVX \leq x^{\text{max}}) \geq 1 - \varepsilon$. It is enforced through the moment-based reformulation,
\begin{IEEEeqnarray}{ r C l }
    \Expectation(\RVX) + \lambda(\varepsilon)\sqrt{\Variance(\RVX)} \leq x^{\text{max}}, \label{eq:cc_constraint}
\end{IEEEeqnarray}
with the expectation and variance of an approximated random variable~$\ApproxRVX$ given in terms of its coefficients as,
\begin{IEEEeqnarray}{ r C l }
    \Expectation[\ApproxRVX]   &=& \CoefficientX_{0}, \label{eq:gpc_expectation} \\
    \Variance[\ApproxRVX]      &=& \mkern-10mu \sum_{\PCEIndex \in \PCESet \backslash \{0\}} \mkern-10mu \langle \Polynoom_{\PCEIndex}, \Polynoom_{\PCEIndex} \rangle \, \CoefficientX_{\PCEIndex}^{2}, \label{eq:gpc_variance}
\end{IEEEeqnarray}
so that~\eqref{eq:cc_constraint} is a second-order cone constraint in the coefficients~$\{\CoefficientX_{\PCEIndex}\}_{\PCEIndex \in \PCESet}$. Three choices of the risk factor~$\lambda(\varepsilon)$ are considered:
\begin{enumerate*}
    \item[(i)] the distribution-free Cantelli bound $\lambda(\varepsilon) = \sqrt{(1-\varepsilon)/\varepsilon}$, which guarantees the chance constraint for any distribution of finite variance;
    \item[(ii)] the Gaussian quantile $\lambda(\varepsilon) = \Phi^{-1}(1-\varepsilon)$; and
    \item[(iii)] an empirical, per-constraint factor $\lambda^{*} = (q_{1-\varepsilon}(\RVX)-\Expectation(\RVX))/\sqrt{\Variance(\RVX)}$, calibrated from samples of the constrained quantity and updated with the hosting capacity until convergence.
\end{enumerate*}
\removable{The Cantelli bound is the safe choice when nothing is known about the output distribution; the empirical factor targets the risk level~$\varepsilon$ exactly.}

\subsection{Uncertainty on Angle of Harmonic Current Injection}
\label{sec:harmonic_current_angle_uncertainty}

This section discusses the treatment of the uncertain angles~$\phi_{\Unit,\Harmonic}$ of the harmonic currents injected by the harmonic units~$\Unit \in \HarmonicUnitSet$, for all harmonics~$\Harmonic \in \HarmonicSet$. Throughout, a polynomial basis of degree~$\delta = 2$ is used, which captures the non-linear nature of the power flow equations without significant loss of accuracy~\cite{muhlpfordt_chance_2019}.

\subsubsection{Random Variable per Harmonic Unit}
As shown in Section~\ref{sec:uncertainty}, the phase angles of a converter across the harmonic orders are strongly dependent, and their marginal distributions are well approximated by a single shared Beta distribution with harmonic-specific location and scale. Each harmonic unit~$\Unit$ is therefore assigned one random variable~$\omega_{\Unit} \sim \mathcal{B}(\hat{\alpha},\hat{\beta})$ on~$[0,1]$, which drives its angle at every harmonic through an affine map,
\begin{IEEEeqnarray}{ r C l }
    \phi_{\Unit,\Harmonic} &=& v^{\text{min}}_{\Harmonic} + (v^{\text{max}}_{\Harmonic} - v^{\text{min}}_{\Harmonic})\,\omega_{\Unit} + \Harmonic\,\theta_{\Node(\Unit)}, \label{eq:angle_affine}
\end{IEEEeqnarray}
while the random variables of different units are independent.

\subsubsection{Local Angle Reference}
The angle ranges of Section~\ref{sec:uncertainty} result from a converter model driven by its own terminal voltage, i.e., they are defined relative to the fundamental voltage at the bus~$\Node(\Unit)$ the unit connects to. A shift of the fundamental by its angle~$\theta_{\Node}$ rotates the $\Harmonic$-th harmonic phasor by~$\Harmonic\,\theta_{\Node}$, which gives the last term of~\eqref{eq:angle_affine}. The fundamental angles follow from the fundamental power flow, which is solved first. Since~$\theta_{\Node}$ depends on the operating point and not only on the network topology, units of the same type at different buses inject at different absolute angles: the local angle reference is a physically required source of angle diversity, and it is the reference used throughout this work.

\subsubsection{Converter Types and Shared Random Variables}
Units of different converter types differ in their mean angle. For a phase-controlled converter, a firing delay~$\alpha_{\text{f}}$ delays the current waveform and rotates the $\Harmonic$-th harmonic phasor by~$-\Harmonic\,\alpha_{\text{f}}$. The firing delay follows the operating point, which is common to all units of a type. Each converter type~$t \in \mathcal{T}$ is therefore assigned one additional random variable~$\omega_{t}$, shared by all its units,
\begin{IEEEeqnarray}{ r C l }
    \phi_{\Unit,\Harmonic} &=& v^{\text{min}}_{\Harmonic} + (v^{\text{max}}_{\Harmonic} - v^{\text{min}}_{\Harmonic})\,\omega_{\Unit} + \Harmonic\,\theta_{\Node(\Unit)} \nonumber \\
    && +\: s^{\text{min}}_{t,\Harmonic} + (s^{\text{max}}_{t,\Harmonic} - s^{\text{min}}_{t,\Harmonic})\,\omega_{t}, \label{eq:angle_affine_type}
\end{IEEEeqnarray}
where the type shift~$[s^{\text{min}}_{t,\Harmonic}, s^{\text{max}}_{t,\Harmonic}]$ maps the firing-delay range of type~$t$ onto harmonic~$\Harmonic$. The germ then has dimension~$|\HarmonicUnitSet| + |\mathcal{T}|$, which by~\eqref{eq:cardinality} adds little to the cardinality; an independent firing delay per unit is the special case of one type per unit. The shared random variable preserves the inter-harmonic dependence, as the same~$\omega_{t}$ drives all harmonics.

\subsubsection{Exact Projection of the Trigonometric Functions}
In the deterministic formulation~\cite{vanacker_hosting_2026}, the harmonic current magnitude~$\CurrentMagnitude_{\Unit,\Harmonic}$ is related to its rectangular components through the angle~$\phi_{\Unit,\Harmonic}$,
\begin{IEEEeqnarray}{ l }
        \cos(\phi_{\Unit,\Harmonic}) \, \CurrentMagnitude_{\Unit,\Harmonic} =
        \RealCurrent_{\Unit,\Harmonic}, \quad \forall \Unit \in \HarmonicUnitSet, \Harmonic \in \HarmonicSet,\label{eq:real_harmonic_unit_current} \\
        \sin(\phi_{\Unit,\Harmonic}) \, \CurrentMagnitude_{\Unit,\Harmonic} =
        \ImaginaryCurrent_{\Unit,\Harmonic}, \quad \forall \Unit \in \HarmonicUnitSet, \Harmonic \in \HarmonicSet. \label{eq:imag_harmonic_unit_current}
\end{IEEEeqnarray}
Since the angle~\eqref{eq:angle_affine} is affine in a single random variable, $\cos(\phi_{\Unit,\Harmonic})$ and $\sin(\phi_{\Unit,\Harmonic})$ are functions of~$\omega_{\Unit}$ alone, and their coefficients follow exactly from~\eqref{eq:gpc_expansion} by Gauss quadrature with respect to the Beta measure,
\begin{IEEEeqnarray}{ r C l }
    \cos(\phi_{\Unit,\Harmonic})_{\PCEIndex} &=& \frac{1}{\langle \Polynoom_{\PCEIndex},\Polynoom_{\PCEIndex}\rangle} \sum_{q} w_{q} \cos\big(\phi_{\Unit,\Harmonic}(\xi_{q})\big)\,\Polynoom_{\PCEIndex}(\xi_{q}), \label{eq:trig_quadrature}
\end{IEEEeqnarray}
with nodes~$\xi_{q}$ and weights~$w_{q}$, and analogously for the sine. This replaces the truncated Taylor series used previously, has no expansion point, and leaves only the degree-$\delta$ truncation error. For the type angle~\eqref{eq:angle_affine_type}, the identities $\cos(a+b) = \cos a\cos b - \sin a \sin b$ and $\sin(a+b) = \sin a\cos b + \cos a \sin b$ split the functions into products of functions of the independent random variables~$\omega_{\Unit}$ and~$\omega_{t}$. As the basis is a tensor product, their coefficients are the products of the univariate projections~\eqref{eq:trig_quadrature}, truncated to total degree~$\delta$. In both cases~$\cos(\phi_{\Unit,\Harmonic})_{\PCEIndex}$ and~$\sin(\phi_{\Unit,\Harmonic})_{\PCEIndex}$ are parameters, not optimization variables.

\subsection{General Stochastic Harmonic Hosting Capacity}
\label{sec:stochastic_harmonic_hosting_capacity}

The stochastic model determines a \emph{deterministic} harmonic hosting capacity given uncertainty on the harmonic current angles, while complying with chance constraints on (harmonic) voltages and currents. Given that the problem is formulated from the perspective of the power system operator, deterministic harmonic budgets are the practical outcome for its customers. A deterministic hosting capacity implies that~$\CurrentMagnitude_{\Unit,\Harmonic,\PCEIndex} = 0$ for any non-zero index~$\PCEIndex$, so the counterparts of~\eqref{eq:real_harmonic_unit_current} and~\eqref{eq:imag_harmonic_unit_current} are,
\begin{IEEEeqnarray}{ l }
    \cos(\phi_{\Unit,\Harmonic})_{\PCEIndex} \, \CurrentMagnitude_{\Unit,\Harmonic,0} = \RealCurrent_{\Unit,\Harmonic,\PCEIndex},
        \quad \forall \Unit \in \HarmonicUnitSet, \Harmonic \in \HarmonicSet, \PCEIndex \in \PCESet,
    \label{eq:real_harmonic_unit_current_pce} \\
    \sin(\phi_{\Unit,\Harmonic})_{\PCEIndex} \, \CurrentMagnitude_{\Unit,\Harmonic,0} = \ImaginaryCurrent_{\Unit,\Harmonic,\PCEIndex},
        \quad \forall \Unit \in \HarmonicUnitSet, \Harmonic \in \HarmonicSet, \PCEIndex \in \PCESet,
    \label{eq:imag_harmonic_unit_current_pce}
\end{IEEEeqnarray}
which are \emph{linear} in the decision variables. Any variables related to the fairness objective are also deterministic. All other parameters, e.g., network impedances, are deterministic.

\subsubsection{Reformulation of Linear Constraints}
\label{sec:reformulation_of_linear_constraints}
The network equations of the current-voltage formulation in~\cite{vanacker_hosting_2026}, i.e., the reference bus, Kirchhoff's current law, Ohm's law, branch current and transformer constraints~(10)--(31), are linear, as are the fairness constraints~(34), (36) and~(38)--(39) and the harmonic current injection constraints~(40)--(41), of which the latter are treated above. Supported by~\eqref{eq:gp_sum}, a generic linear constraint $a\,\RVX + b\,\RVY = c$ with deterministic parameters is repeated over all indices, $a\,\RVX_{k} + b\,\RVY_{k} = c_{k}$, $\forall \PCEIndex \in \PCESet$, where $c_{k} = c$ for $k=0$ and $c_{k}=0$ otherwise. Since each voltage and current coefficient is then linear in the injected current coefficients, the physical variables are non-zero only on the indices supported by a single random variable, or by the pair of the random variables of a unit and its type, which reduces the number of variables.

\subsubsection{Reformulation of Quadratic Constraints and Problem Class}
\label{sec:reformulation_of_quadratic_constraints}
The fundamental power limits~(42)--(43) in~\cite{vanacker_hosting_2026} involve only the deterministic fundamental and are retained unchanged. The root-mean-square, total and individual harmonic distortion voltage limits and the root-mean-square current limit are the second-order cone constraints~(47)--(49) and~(51) in~\cite{vanacker_hosting_2026}. Consider a generic constraint $\RVX^{2} + \RVY^{2} \leq a^{2}$ with deterministic~$a$. Following~\cite{vanacker_ivr}, an auxiliary lifted variable~$\RVZ$ is introduced, $\RVX^{2} + \RVY^{2} = \RVZ \leq a^{2}$. Supported by~\eqref{eq:gp_product}, the lifting yields~$|\PCESet|$ constraints,
\begin{IEEEeqnarray}{ l }
    \sum_{\bar{\kappa} \in P^{*}(\PCESet,2)} \mkern-15mu M_{\bar{\kappa},\PCEIndex} \,(\RVX_{\kappa_{1}} \RVX_{\kappa_{2}} + \RVY_{\kappa_{1}} \RVY_{\kappa_{2}}) = \RVZ_{\PCEIndex}, \quad \forall \PCEIndex \in \PCESet. \label{eq:soc_aux_eq}
\end{IEEEeqnarray}
For~$\PCEIndex = 0$ the quadratic form is positive semidefinite, but for~$\PCEIndex \neq 0$ it is indefinite and the equality must be retained: relaxing it would allow~$\RVZ_k = 0$ where the true coefficient is negative, which underestimates the variance and makes the chance constraint over-optimistic. The right-hand side becomes the chance constraint,
\begin{IEEEeqnarray}{ l }
    \RVZ_{0} + \lambda(\varepsilon) \sqrt{\mkern-10mu \sum_{\PCEIndex \in \PCESet \backslash \{0\}} \mkern-10mu \langle \Polynoom_{\PCEIndex}, \Polynoom_{\PCEIndex} \rangle \, \RVZ_{\PCEIndex}^{2}} \leq a^{2}, \label{eq:cc_soc}
\end{IEEEeqnarray}
a second-order cone in~$\{\RVZ_{\PCEIndex}\}_{\PCEIndex\in\PCESet}$. With the equalities~\eqref{eq:soc_aux_eq} for~$\PCEIndex \neq 0$, the general stochastic harmonic hosting capacity problem is a non-convex quadratically constrained quadratic program.

\subsection{Second-Order Cone Program for Absolute Equality}
\label{sec:absolute_equality_socp}

Under the absolute equality fairness criterion, all harmonic units share a single hosting capacity~$I_{\Harmonic}$ per harmonic. The network equations are linear in the coefficients, and~\eqref{eq:real_harmonic_unit_current_pce}--\eqref{eq:imag_harmonic_unit_current_pce} express every injected current coefficient as a known parameter times~$I_{\Harmonic}$, so all voltage coefficients are linear in~$I_{\Harmonic}$,
\begin{IEEEeqnarray}{ l }
    \RealVoltage_{\Node,\Harmonic,\PCEIndex} = C^{\text{re}}_{\Node,\Harmonic,\PCEIndex}\, I_{\Harmonic}, \qquad
    \ImaginaryVoltage_{\Node,\Harmonic,\PCEIndex} = C^{\text{im}}_{\Node,\Harmonic,\PCEIndex}\, I_{\Harmonic},
    \label{eq:voltage_linear}
\end{IEEEeqnarray}
where~$C^{\text{re}}$ and~$C^{\text{im}}$ follow from the harmonic transfer impedances~$Z_{\Harmonic}$ of the network, obtained by inverting the harmonic admittance matrix, and the trigonometric coefficients~\eqref{eq:trig_quadrature}; branch currents follow analogously. The lifting then collapses to the precomputed scalars,
\begin{IEEEeqnarray}{ l }
\alpha_{\Node,\Harmonic,\PCEIndex} = \mkern-12mu \sum_{\bar{\kappa} \in P^{*}(\PCESet,2)} \mkern-12mu M_{\bar{\kappa},\PCEIndex} \big( C^{\text{re}}_{\Node,\Harmonic,\bar{\kappa}_{1}} C^{\text{re}}_{\Node,\Harmonic,\bar{\kappa}_{2}} \nonumber \\
\qquad\qquad +\: C^{\text{im}}_{\Node,\Harmonic,\bar{\kappa}_{1}} C^{\text{im}}_{\Node,\Harmonic,\bar{\kappa}_{2}} \big),
\end{IEEEeqnarray}
i.e., the coefficients of~$|\ComplexVoltage_{\Node,\Harmonic}|^2/I_{\Harmonic}^2$. With~$g_{\Harmonic} \geq I_{\Harmonic}^{2}$, a rotated second-order cone, every chance constraint on a sum of squared harmonic quantities over a set~$\mathcal{H}_{c} \subseteq \HarmonicSet$ of harmonics becomes,
\begin{IEEEeqnarray}{ l }
    \sum_{\Harmonic \in \mathcal{H}_{c}} \alpha_{\Node,\Harmonic,0}\, g_{\Harmonic} \leq a_{\Node,c} \nonumber \\
    \quad -\: \lambda(\varepsilon) \sqrt{\mkern-10mu \sum_{\PCEIndex \in \PCESet \backslash \{0\}} \mkern-10mu \langle \Polynoom_{\PCEIndex}, \Polynoom_{\PCEIndex} \rangle \Big( \sum_{\Harmonic \in \mathcal{H}_{c}} \alpha_{\Node,\Harmonic,\PCEIndex}\, g_{\Harmonic} \Big)^{2}}, \label{eq:socp_cc}
\end{IEEEeqnarray}
a second-order cone in~$\bar{g}$: $\mathcal{H}_{c} = \{\Harmonic\}$ for the individual harmonic voltage distortion, which reduces to a linear bound on~$g_{\Harmonic}$, and $\mathcal{H}_{c} = \HarmonicSet$ for the total harmonic distortion and the root-mean-square voltage and current limits, where~$a_{\Node,c}$ is the limit net of the fundamental. The stochastic harmonic hosting capacity under absolute equality is thus the second-order cone program,
\begin{IEEEeqnarray}{ r C l }
    \max_{\bar{I},\,\bar{g}} \; \sum_{\Harmonic \in \HarmonicSet} |\HarmonicUnitSet|\, I_{\Harmonic} \;\; \text{s.t.} \;\; I_{\Harmonic}^{2} \leq g_{\Harmonic},\; I_{\Harmonic} \leq I^{\text{rated}},\; \eqref{eq:socp_cc}, \label{eq:socp}
\end{IEEEeqnarray}
with one pair of variables per harmonic, whatever the size of the network or the cardinality of the basis; the latter only enters the precomputed scalars~$\alpha$. A limit that the fundamental already exhausts, $a_{\Node,c} \leq 0$, leaves no harmonic headroom and forces the contributing~$g_{\Harmonic}$ to zero. When every angle range collapses to a point, all variances vanish and~\eqref{eq:socp} reduces exactly to the deterministic model.

\subsection{Quantifying Harmonic Recombination}
\label{sec:recombination_metrics}

Harmonic recombination is the reduction of the aggregate distortion below the arithmetic sum of the individual contributions. Under absolute equality, the binding constraints are typically the individual harmonic voltage limits (see Section~\ref{sec:numerical_illustration}), so that at harmonic~$\Harmonic$ the hosting capacity is set by the tightest bus,
\begin{IEEEeqnarray}{ r C l }
    I_{\Harmonic} &=& \min_{\Node \in \NodeSet} \frac{U^{\text{ihd}}_{\Node,\Harmonic}\,|\ComplexVoltage_{\Node,1}|}{A_{\Node,\Harmonic}}, \qquad c_{\Node\Unit,\Harmonic} = Z_{\Harmonic,\Node\Node(\Unit)}\,e^{\im\phi_{\Unit,\Harmonic}}, \label{eq:reduced_hosting}
\end{IEEEeqnarray}
where~$A_{\Node,\Harmonic}$ aggregates the unit contributions~$c_{\Node\Unit,\Harmonic}$ per unit of current. Four aggregation rules are compared:
\begin{enumerate*}
    \item[(i)] arithmetic summation $\sum_{\Unit}|c_{\Node\Unit,\Harmonic}|$, i.e., no recombination;
    \item[(ii)] the robust bound $\max_{\bar{\omega}}|\sum_{\Unit}c_{\Node\Unit,\Harmonic}|$ over the support of the germ, evaluated exactly through the support function of the angle box;
    \item[(iii)] the deterministic angle $|\sum_{\Unit}c_{\Node\Unit,\Harmonic}(\Expectation[\bar\omega])|$, as in~\cite{vanacker_hosting_2026}; and
    \item[(iv)] the $(1-\varepsilon)$-quantile of $|\sum_{\Unit}c_{\Node\Unit,\Harmonic}|$, i.e., the exact per-constraint chance bound.
\end{enumerate*}
The uplift of~(iv) over~(ii) is the hosting capacity a chance constraint gains over a safe deterministic bound. To relate the result to IEC~61000-3-6~\cite{noauthor_iec61000-3-6_2008}, the \emph{equivalent summation exponent}~$\alpha^{\text{eq}}_{\Harmonic}$ is defined as the exponent for which the general summation law,
\begin{IEEEeqnarray}{ r C l }
    A_{\Node,\Harmonic} &=& \Big(\sum_{\Unit \in \HarmonicUnitSet} |c_{\Node\Unit,\Harmonic}|^{\alpha^{\text{eq}}_{\Harmonic}}\Big)^{1/\alpha^{\text{eq}}_{\Harmonic}}, \label{eq:summation_law}
\end{IEEEeqnarray}
reproduces the network hosting capacity~\eqref{eq:reduced_hosting} of rule~(iv); $\alpha^{\text{eq}}_{\Harmonic} = 1$ is arithmetic summation, and the standard's indicative values are~$1.4$ for~$5 \leq \Harmonic \leq 10$ and~$2$ for~$\Harmonic > 10$.
