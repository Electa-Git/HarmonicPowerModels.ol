\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

\subsection{Background and Motivation}
\label{sec:background_and_motivation}

\IEEEPARstart{R}{eaching} a net-zero economy by 2050 requires extensive electrification of the energy system. During the past two decades, renewable generation, particularly wind and photovoltaic power, has grown steadily and is expected to accelerate towards 2050~\cite{international_energy_agency_world_2023}. At the same time, decarbonization targets require the electrification of transport, heating, and industrial processes, replacing fossil fuels with electricity. In Europe, studies project that the total electricity demand will triple by 2050~\cite{energyville_paths_2023}. Consequently, future power systems will be largely dominated by power-electronic interfaced resources on both the supply and demand sides. This transition fundamentally changes the harmonic behavior of power systems. Traditional grids, dominated by synchronous machines and mostly linear loads, exhibited limited harmonic distortion. Modern systems, however, increasingly rely on converters whose nonlinear characteristics generate a broad spectrum of harmonic currents. The aggregation of numerous distributed converters creates new challenges in maintaining harmonic voltage quality across transmission and distribution networks.

A key challenge arises from the stochastic nature of harmonic emissions. Measurements and laboratory studies show that harmonic magnitudes depend on loading, switching states, and internal control actions. More importantly, harmonic current phase angles, which govern the phasor summation of emissions from multiple sources, vary significantly. Even small angle variations can result in large differences in harmonic voltage distortion, particularly in networks with many distributed converters. As the penetration of the converter increases, system operators need transparent metrics to assess whether additional units can be connected without exceeding harmonic limits. Harmonic hosting capacity provides such a metric by defining the maximum allowable harmonic emissions while ensuring compliance with power-quality standards. However, most existing methods rely on deterministic assumptions regarding harmonic magnitudes, phase angles, network parameters, and background distortion, leading to overly conservative or insufficiently protective results.

\textcolor{blue}{Likewise, the harmonic impedance of the network is uncertain. Network models of system operators describe cables and overhead lines by their sequence resistance, reactance and susceptance at the fundamental frequency, taken from datasheets or commissioning measurements, and harmonic studies extrapolate these values to higher frequencies with scaling rules. The harmonic impedance, however, follows from the physical inputs of each cable and line: the resistivities and temperatures of the conductors and screens, the permittivity of the insulation, the dimensions within their manufacturing tolerances, the laying geometry and the earth. Most of these inputs depend on frequency and temperature, and ``such data are not available even from a cable manufacturer. The same is true for the earth resistivity and permittivity''~\cite[Sec.~2.5.2.6]{ametani_cable_2015}. A cable project adds its own variability: the conductor temperature follows the load, the earth resistivity varies by orders of magnitude along the route and over the seasons, and the installed cables depart from their design within the acceptance limits of the standards. The harmonic impedances near a parallel resonance are the most sensitive to these inputs, and the scaling rules miss their joint effect. On the IEEE 30-bus system of Section~\ref{sec:numerical_illustration}, the hosting capacity allocated on the scaled fundamental parameters drives a squared harmonic quantity of the physical model up to 9.7 times its limit, and a robust allocation over scaling factors derived from the same physical inputs still violates the limits at every sampled network. The harmonic hosting capacity must therefore hold for every network that the physical inputs allow.}

\subsubsection{Harmonic hosting capacity}

The standard IEC~61000-3-6 allocates planning levels over the installations connected to a point of common coupling, aggregates their emissions with a summation law whose exponents are indicative, and compares the 95\,\% distortion values with the planning levels~\cite{noauthor_iec61000-3-6_2008}. Santos et al.\ used the summation law to determine the harmonic hosting capacity behind a Norton equivalent with background distortion of unknown phase, and obtained between 37\,MW and 160\,MW for the in-phase and anti-phase extremes~\cite{santos_considerations_2015}. Sun and Harrison proposed the first optimization-based stochastic method, with percentile-based harmonic distortion limits as chance constraints within an ac optimal power flow~\cite{sun_distribution_2022}. Nakhodchi et al.\ included the measured emission uncertainty of a fast charging station for electric vehicles~\cite{nakhodchi2022}, and showed that the estimated hosting capacity depends strongly on how the medium-voltage network and the remote loads are modeled~\cite{nakhodchi_impact_2023}. Ismael et al.\ maximized the harmonic-constrained hosting capacity of a photovoltaic system by designing a C-type filter with a metaheuristic wrapped around a Monte Carlo simulation of 1000 samples, with tolerances on the loads, the harmonic currents, the background voltage and the filter resistance and reactance, and found a probabilistic hosting capacity 39.7\,\% above the deterministic one~\cite{ismael_probabilistic_2019}. At transmission level, a Monte Carlo study over the system loading found the harmonic hosting capacity to be governed by resonance-sensitive impedance~\cite{emini_study_2026}. The present authors determined an exact lower bound for the equitable harmonic hosting capacity with a second-order cone formulation of the full network~\cite{vanacker_hosting_2026}, showed that underground cables change it substantially~\cite{vanacker_impact_2025}, and extended it to stochastic harmonic current angles with chance constraints~\cite{vanacker_shhc}. Except for the filter in~\cite{ismael_probabilistic_2019}, whose parameters are sampled within 1\,\%, all of these methods take the network parameters as known.

\subsubsection{Uncertainty of the network harmonic impedance}

The harmonic impedance that sets the hosting capacity is, however, uncertain. The joint CIGRE and CIRED guide stresses that it changes continuously with load and network configuration, recommends representative operating conditions or worst-case impedance curves, and limits the validity of network models to about the 20th harmonic~\cite{robert_guide_1997}. Testa et al.\ built a probabilistic model of the nodal impedances of the 13-bus IEEE industrial test system from a 5\,\% tolerance on the inductances and capacitances and the variability of the load and supply configuration, and concluded that harmonic voltage assessments that ignore the impedance variability, or its correlation with the injected currents, give unreliable results~\cite{testa_probabilistic_2002}. Li et al.\ attributed the parameter uncertainty of lines, transformers and capacitors to manufacturing variation, temperature and aging, propagated a variation of 10\,\% through resonance mode analysis~\cite{xu_harmonic_2005,huang_application_2007} by Monte Carlo simulation, and obtained probabilistic resonance frequency bands and modal impedances instead of single resonance points~\cite{li_probabilistic_2018}; the same group separated aleatory from epistemic sources and defined amplification indices~\cite{li2020}. For wind farms, Zhao and Milanovi\'c showed by Monte Carlo simulation, which includes the availability of turbines and cables, that resonances appear with the plant configuration and the short-circuit level of the external grid~\cite{zhao_equivalent_2022}. For two offshore connections in the Netherlands, Barakou et al.\ found that network and plant contingencies dominate the harmonic amplification, that the choice of cable model is noticeable, a geometry-based model being recommended over the distributed $\pi$-model, and that a manufacturing tolerance of 8\,\% on the cable capacitance has a minor effect~\cite{barakou_parameter_2023}. Babaev et al.\ argued that a theoretical derivation of the cable harmonic impedance neglects the operating conditions, configuration and installation of the cable, and estimated it online with an uncertainty budget~\cite{babaev_online_2020}. These works sample or scan the network parameters, but none of them optimizes the hosting capacity over their uncertainty.

\subsubsection{Harmonic analysis under bounded uncertainty}

Where probability distributions are not available, uncertainty is described by bounds. Romero et al.\ introduced the harmonic load flow based on possibility theory~\cite{romero_harmonic_2011} and extended the frequency scan with it: triangular possibility distributions of the load composition and magnitude yield the upper boundary of the driving-point impedance and the possibility and necessity of a parallel resonance~\cite{romero_frequency_2018}. Interval arithmetic provides strict bounds on the power flow solution for interval nodal data, at the price of conservatism~\cite{wang_interval_1992}, which affine arithmetic reduces by tracking the dependency between the uncertain quantities~\cite{vaccaro_affine_2010}. Both were carried to harmonics: Huang et al.\ proposed a Newton-Raphson interval harmonic power flow in affine form~\cite{huang_newton_2017}, Wang et al.\ a complex affine three-phase harmonic power flow that traces the contribution of each distributed generator~\cite{wang_tracing_2018}, and de Sousa et al.\ an interval harmonic power flow for microgrids based on a second-order Taylor expansion, whose intervals enclose those of a Monte Carlo simulation~\cite{desousa_interval_2024}. In all of these, the bounded quantities are the injections of generators, loads and harmonic sources, while the network admittance is fixed. Lin et al.\ are the exception: from the extended Gersgorin theorem, they derived a sufficient condition on the elements of the harmonic admittance matrix that keeps it away from singularity, i.e., a parameter domain that is free of harmonic resonance, which is conservative by construction~\cite{lin_extended_2017}. These methods bound a response or a feasible domain for given injections, but do not determine the hosting capacity.

\subsubsection{Robust optimization}

Robust optimization requires a constraint to hold for every realization of uncertain data within a set, and replaces the uncertain program by its robust counterpart, which is tractable for linear programs with ellipsoidal sets~\cite{ben-tal_robust_1999}. Where no tractable counterpart exists, cutting-set methods alternate between a master problem over finite scenario sets and a worst-case analysis~\cite{mutapcic_cutting-set_2009}. In power systems, robust optimization has mainly addressed the uncertainty of renewable generation and load~\cite{roald_power_2023}, e.g., in the two-stage robust ac optimal power flow~\cite{louca_robust_2019}, its robust convex restriction, which names the impedance parameters as future work~\cite{lee_robust_2021}, the robust chance-constrained optimal power flow~\cite{lubin_robust_2016}, and the hosting capacity for distributed generation under budgeted load and generation sets~\cite{wang_distributed_2016}. Few works place the network itself in the uncertainty set. Christakou et al.\ controlled the voltage of a distribution network with line resistances that are bounded but of unknown distribution, due to conductor temperature and incomplete asset data, and kept the robust counterpart a mixed-integer linear program by linearizing the network with sensitivity coefficients~\cite{christakou_voltage_2018}. Liu and Ma determined the robust feasible region of distributed energy resources for interval line impedances in a linear unbalanced power flow, which already leads to a nonconvex bilinear problem~\cite{liu_feasible_2023}, and Mirbagheri et al.\ estimated the hosting capacity of feeders whose data are uncertain or missing from classes of representative feeders~\cite{mirbagheri_hosting_2018}. All of these are linear or linearized models at the fundamental frequency, whereas the network parameters enter the harmonic hosting capacity through the inverse of the harmonic admittance matrix, which is far from concave near a parallel resonance.

\subsubsection{Research gaps}

Table~\ref{tab:literature_review} shows that tractable network-wide optimization with an explicit treatment of the uncertainty of the harmonic current angles and chance-constraint enforcement of the harmonic distortion limits is available~\cite{vanacker_shhc}, that the uncertainty of the network harmonic impedance is sampled or scanned but not optimized over, and that the bounded-uncertainty harmonic methods bound the injections rather than the network. No existing method simultaneously achieves:
\begin{enumerate*}
    \item [(i)] an optimization of the harmonic hosting capacity with a guarantee over the uncertainty of the network parameters, i.e., robustly rather than by sampling,
    \item [(ii)] network parameters derived from the physical construction of the cables and lines and their frequency dependence, rather than from scaled fundamental-frequency data, and
    \item [(iii)] the joint treatment of both uncertainties, the angles in probability and the network in the worst case.
\end{enumerate*}
Moreover, the zero-sequence harmonics, for which the transformer windings and earthing set the network, are absent from the optimization-based methods.

\subsection{Contributions and Outline}
\label{sec:contribution_and_outline}

In this paper, \textcolor{blue}{the stochastic harmonic hosting capacity of~\cite{vanacker_shhc} is extended to a phase-balanced three-phase power system whose cable and overhead-line parameters are uncertain}. The presented method considers uncertainty on the angle of the injected harmonic current, enabling harmonic recombination, and limits the resulting individual and total harmonic voltage distortion, as well as the root-mean-square voltages and currents, using chance constraints. The problem is formulated from the perspective of the power system operator, who may anticipate the converter technologies its customers will connect, but not the operating point, parameters and control of the individual units. The angle of the harmonic current is therefore modeled as a random variable per unit, characterized for the anticipated technology, whereas its magnitude is the decision, and the harmonic hosting capacity is determined in terms of harmonic current magnitude using a fairness principle. \textcolor{blue}{The cable and overhead-line parameters follow from physical inputs, e.g., conductor temperatures, manufacturing tolerances and the earth resistivity, that are known to lie in intervals, so the hosting capacity must hold for every network in that set.} The presented formulation uses the rectangular current-voltage variable space and incorporates:
\begin{itemize}
    \item uncertainty on the angle of the injected harmonic current via polynomial chaos expansion,
    \item \textcolor{blue}{series impedance and shunt admittance of the cables and overhead lines computed at every harmonic frequency from their physical inputs, with intervals from the acceptance limits of the standards and the operating ranges,}
    \item exact representation of the harmonic impedance in the frequency domain and detailed treatment of transformers\textcolor{blue}{, in the zero, positive and negative sequence},
    \item enforcement of stochastic harmonic limits on bus voltage and component ampacity through chance constraints,
    \item \textcolor{blue}{the robust counterpart of these chance constraints over the parameter set, solved by a cutting-set method that gives a robust lower bound and an upper bound on the hosting capacity,} and
    \item equitable distribution of the harmonic hosting capacity following a fairness principle.
\end{itemize}
For fairness criteria that fix the ratios between the allocations of the units, e.g., absolute equality, \textcolor{blue}{and a constant risk factor, the robust counterpart is a convex semi-infinite second-order cone program} with one pair of variables per harmonic. %
The models and numerical illustrations are made freely available as part of the \textsc{Julia}-package \textsc{HarmonicPowerModels.jl}\footnote{Available at \url{https://github.com/timmyfaraday/HarmonicPowerModels.jl}.}\textcolor{blue}{, with the cable and line parameters from \textsc{LineCableModels.jl}~\cite{lcm_2026}}.

Section~\ref{sec:numerical_illustration} compares the risk factors and applies the method to the IEEE 30-bus system with physical cable and line inputs, including the zero-sequence harmonics and the transformer earthing.} Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes the paper.
